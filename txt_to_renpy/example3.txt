The heat wave had turned the apartment into a veritable oven. After visiting several stores and enduring long queues, my wife and I returned home convinced that the fan we had just bought would save our afternoon. The moment we took it out of the box and plugged it in, however, it emitted a strange buzzing sound. The blades turned for barely a second before the motor died with a sharp click. Within moments, the unpleasant smell of overheated plastic began to spread through the living room.

``I can't believe this had to happen today of all days. We've spent the whole morning walking around in unbearable heat, paid a small fortune because we wanted a decent-quality fan, and when we finally get home thinking we can relax for a while, we find out the thing is broken. It's so frustrating—it looked like the perfect solution to this heat wave.

``And the worst part is that the salesman kept insisting it was one of the most reliable models they sold and that they almost never had returns. Now we'll have to go all the way back, stand in another endless queue, and argue over something that should never have happened in the first place. Frankly, I find it unacceptable that a brand-new product should fail before it's even had the chance to do its job.´´

My wife unplugged the fan and carefully examined the cord, the blades, and the switch, although the expression on her face made it clear she wasn't expecting to find an obvious explanation.

"I followed the instructions exactly as they appear in the manual. I even checked twice that every single part had been assembled correctly before plugging it in, precisely so no one could later claim the problem was somehow our fault.

"Besides, it's disrespectful to customers to sell an appliance that can't even survive its first few seconds of operation. When you buy a household appliance, you expect at least a basic standard of quality—not to waste your time taking it apart, packing it up again, and returning to the store on the very day you bought it."

I walked over to the fan and flicked the switch once more. The result was exactly the same: a brief attempt to start, followed immediately by silence.

"Do you know what we're going to do? We're keeping absolutely everything: the box, the plastic bags, the manual, the spare screws, and, of course, the receipt. I don't want them finding the slightest excuse to refuse us a replacement or a refund. If I have to speak to the manager, I'll do it calmly—but I'll also make my point perfectly clear.

"And this time I'm not leaving the store until I've seen the replacement working properly right there in front of me. I'm not about to carry another box halfway across the city only to get home and discover that the second fan has exactly the same problem. We've been patient enough already with this heat," I said angrily.

My wife nodded slowly. Together we packed the fan back into its original box. Outside, the air remained perfectly still, and the heat continued to pour through the open windows. I sealed the box carefully and lifted it off the floor.

''We'd better leave as soon as possible. If we wait much longer, we'll arrive at the store in an even worse mood than we're already in." Her tone was difficult to read. Was she angry? ". And knowing us, that won't do anyone any favours. Let's just hope they replace it without any fuss, because if they try to convince us this kind of fault is normal, I'm afraid the conversation is going to last much longer than any of them would like."

Without another word, we left the apartment with the box tucked under my arm. The heat was still oppressive, but at least we agreed on one thing: that defective fan wasn't going to have the final say.
