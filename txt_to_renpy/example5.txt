I'll never forget the morning I arrived at my uncle's small farm. The veterinarian was already there, examining the herd with unusual seriousness. The cows were restless, pacing in circles and flinching at every little sound.

"Do you really think it could be mad cow disease?" I asked, feeling a knot tighten in my stomach.

"It's still too early to be certain," the veterinarian replied. "But some of the symptoms fit, and we need to be extremely cautious."

My uncle took off his cap and stared toward the barn for a long moment.

"We've been noticing strange behavior for weeks. Some of the cows seem disoriented, while others startle for no apparent reason. We've never seen anything like it before.

"Yesterday, one of them kept walking in tight circles for several minutes. At first we thought a sudden noise had frightened her, but then we realized this wasn't normal behavior. I truly hope we're wrong, because the consequences could be very serious."

The veterinarian collected several samples and placed them carefully inside his case.

"We'll have to send these to the lab as soon as possible." He closed the case, scribbled a few notes in a small notebook, and looked back at the herd before adding, "In the meantime, the safest course of action is to isolate the animals showing symptoms."

"I know," the veterinarian said. "But delaying action could put every other animal at risk and make the situation much worse."

For a while, we stood in silence as one of the cows kept stamping at the ground.

"What if it turns out to be something else?" I asked, clinging to a small measure of hope.

"Then we'll all breathe a sigh of relief," the veterinarian replied with a faint smile. "Believe me, I'd rather be wrong a thousand times than confirm a diagnosis like this."

Just then, one of the cows let out a long, unsettling moo.

"Did you hear that...?"

My uncle turned sharply toward the barn.

"I've never heard one sound like that before."

The three of us approached cautiously. The cow stood perfectly still, staring into the distance with vacant eyes.

"Whatever the cause may be," the veterinarian said as he carefully observed the animal, "we need to find out as soon as possible. The rest of the herd depends on it."

I left the farm with the feeling that the morning had lasted far longer than the clock suggested. As I walked away, I realized that the true burden of a disease lies not only in its symptoms, but also in the uncertainty it leaves behind.
